Methods

RISA
Rotation-Invariant Sparse Attention. Uses angle/distance features + sparse attention with a global encoding token. Fully SO(3) invariant by design.
DGCNN
Dynamic Graph CNN. Builds a k-NN graph on point features and applies edge convolutions. Not rotation invariant without augmentation.
PointNet++
Hierarchical point set learning with set abstraction layers. Strong baseline but sensitive to rotation without augmentation.
DiPVNet
Disentangled Point-Voxel Network. Combines point and voxel representations for richer geometry encoding.
RINet
Rotation-Invariant Network using local reference frames. Achieves invariance via LRF construction rather than attention.
3D-SIFT
3D Scale-Invariant Feature Transform with inverted index retrieval. Hand-crafted descriptor baseline.

Paper Summary

RISA addresses the critical challenge of rotation sensitivity in 3D point cloud retrieval by introducing a rotation-invariant sparse attention mechanism that ensures consistent performance regardless of object orientation. The experimental validation begins with fundamental robustness checks, where Experiment 1 demonstrates that RISA maintains high R@1 accuracy across 19 SO(3) rotation angles on ShapeNet, significantly outperforming baselines like PointNet++ and DGCNN. This global robustness is further substantiated by Experiment 2, where t-SNE visualizations of 512-dimensional embeddings reveal well-separated clusters for 1280 shapes, indicating that the model learns discriminative and semantically coherent features. To isolate the source of this performance, Experiment 3 conducts an architecture ablation on ModelNet40, confirming that both the SO(3) augmentation and the encoding token are essential, while raw XYZ inputs degrade performance. Building on this architectural insight, Experiment 4 evaluates the generalizability of the Perceiver-based variant, showing that RISA with `perceiver=True` retains superior accuracy across all rotation angles when transferring from ModelNet40 to the more challenging ScanObjectNN dataset. The scope of validation then expands to fine-grained tasks in Experiment 8, where RISA achieves competitive class and part mAP@5 scores in part-level retrieval, proving its utility beyond whole-object identification. Finally, Experiment 10 investigates efficiency and feature selection by comparing seven point selection priors, revealing that the learned `salient_K` prior offers the best trade-off between accuracy and computational cost compared to static methods like FPS or curvature. Collectively, these experiments construct a compelling narrative from basic retrieval robustness to architectural necessity and fine-grained applicability, with the consistent superiority of RISA in both global and part-level tasks serving as the strongest evidence of its efficacy. However, the reliance on specific augmentation strategies and the need for learned priors suggest that further research is required to fully automate the selection process and reduce training overhead for real-time applications.

Analysis

Tight, well-separated clusters in t-SNE visualizations indicate that a model possesses high discriminative power, effectively mapping semantically similar shapes into proximate regions while maintaining distinct boundaries between different classes. Conversely, semantic collapse occurs when embeddings from distinct classes overlap significantly, which destroys retrieval precision by causing the model to confuse unrelated shapes and return irrelevant results during nearest-neighbor searches. Non-invariant architectures like PointNet++ and DGCNN are expected to exhibit this collapse more frequently because their feature extraction is sensitive to orientation, causing rotated instances of the same shape to scatter across the embedding space and blur class boundaries. In contrast, RISA’s cluster structure reveals that its rotation-invariant sparse attention mechanism stabilizes the embedding space geometry, ensuring that rotational variations do not disrupt the compactness of class-specific regions. This geometric stability directly correlates with superior performance in downstream tasks, as the clear separation observed in t-SNE mirrors the high mAP@5 retrieval metrics achieved by RISA, confirming that robust spatial clustering translates to accurate and reliable shape identification.

Analysis

The full RISA model (A) achieves the highest performance with a 95.0% R@1@180, significantly outperforming the worst-performing condition, DGCNN with test-time averaging (C), by a margin of 23.9 percentage points at the 180-degree rotation. The substantial drop in performance from A (95.0%) to D (58.2%) when replacing rotation-invariant features with raw XYZ coordinates reveals that the backbone architecture alone cannot compensate for the lack of invariant input representations, highlighting that feature-level invariance is more critical than architectural complexity for robust retrieval. Augmentation-based approaches like B and C are not competitive with RISA, as B suffers from a 5-point drop at 180 degrees and C fails catastrophically, demonstrating that relying on data augmentation or test-time averaging introduces significant computational overhead without guaranteeing consistent invariance across all rotations. This ablation confirms the key architectural insight that explicit rotation-invariant feature engineering, combined with a global aggregation mechanism, is superior to implicit invariance learned through augmentation or post-hoc averaging for achieving high-precision 3D point cloud retrieval.

Analysis

The standard self-attention variant (perceiver=False) demonstrates superior cross-dataset transfer performance, outperforming the perceiver architecture by a margin of 3.7% at zero rotation (37.8% vs. 34.1% R@1). While the perceiver’s fixed latent queries theoretically act as a noise-filtering bottleneck that could regularize features against sensor artifacts, this compression appears to discard subtle geometric details necessary for distinguishing real-world objects, thereby hurting generalization. The significant performance drop from synthetic training to real-world testing highlights the substantial sim-to-real gap, where factors like non-uniform point density and occlusion challenge features learned on clean CAD models. Rotation invariance proves less effective in the noisy real-world domain because sensor noise and missing data disrupt the geometric symmetries that invariant features rely upon to generalize. Analysis of the per-angle R@1 curves suggests that the perceiver variant degrades more rapidly as rotation increases, indicating that its bottlenecked representation is less robust to the compounded difficulty of rotation combined with domain shift. Consequently, deploying retrieval systems on real sensor data such as LiDAR or RGB-D scans requires architectures that preserve high-fidelity point interactions rather than aggressively compressing them into fixed latent spaces. Ultimately, these results suggest that for robust 3D retrieval in the wild, maintaining detailed local feature representations is more critical than the computational efficiency or density invariance offered by perceiver-style bottlenecks.
